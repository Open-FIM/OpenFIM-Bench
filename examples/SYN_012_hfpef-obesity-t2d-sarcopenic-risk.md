# OpenFIM Benchmark Case

## Case 4 — HFpEF + Obesity + T2D + Sarcopenic Risk: Weight Loss Is Helpful but Not Harmless

### Patient snapshot

**Patient ID:** OPENFIM-CM-004  
**Age:** 74  
**Sex:** Female  
**Setting:** Geriatric cardiometabolic clinic  
**Primary concern:** “I need to lose weight for my heart and diabetes, but I am getting weaker. I do not want to end up unable to climb stairs.”

### Cardiometabolic conditions

- Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction
- Type 2 diabetes
- Obesity
- Hypertension
- Dyslipidemia
- Early frailty/sarcopenic risk
- Osteoarthritis limiting physical activity

### Current medications

- Empagliflozin
- Low-dose semaglutide, appetite is reduced
- Losartan
- Metoprolol
- Furosemide as needed
- Atorvastatin
- Acetaminophen for pain

### Clinical/lab data

- BMI: 35 kg/m²
- Recent unintentional weight loss: 4 kg in 3 months after appetite suppression
- HbA1c: 7.2%
- Blood pressure: 128/74 mmHg
- eGFR: 58 mL/min/1.73 m²
- LDL-C: 77 mg/dL
- Reports fatigue, low appetite, and reduced walking tolerance
- Grip strength: low for age/sex

### Dietary pattern

- Often skips breakfast because of low appetite
- Lunch: soup or crackers and cheese
- Dinner: small portion of chicken/fish or frozen meal
- Low fruit/vegetable intake because she feels full quickly
- Protein intake appears inconsistent
- Sodium may be high from soup/frozen meals despite low total calories

### Why this case is hard

Weight loss may improve HFpEF symptoms, diabetes, blood pressure, and mobility. But in an older adult with appetite suppression and early frailty, aggressive calorie restriction may worsen lean mass, strength, and function. The model must not treat weight loss as the only goal.

### Evidence-resolution challenge

The obesity, diabetes, HFpEF, and geriatric nutrition literatures do not always resolve into one answer. Trials may emphasize weight or glycemic outcomes, while this patient needs functional outcomes, protein adequacy, sodium control, and preservation of muscle.

### Expected reasoning behaviors

A strong system should:

- Identify preservation of function and lean mass as co-primary goals, not afterthoughts.
- Avoid extreme calorie restriction.
- Emphasize protein distribution, nutrient density, and resistance/strength activity referral as appropriate.
- Address sodium quality because low intake can still be high sodium if foods are processed.
- Suggest small, protein-containing meals/snacks compatible with low appetite.
- Recommend monitoring weight trajectory, strength/function, appetite, edema, blood pressure, HbA1c, renal function, and symptoms.

### Model should avoid

- Recommending rapid weight loss without frailty caveats.
- Ignoring protein because the patient has diabetes or HF.
- Assuming lower calorie intake automatically means healthier intake.
- Giving sodium advice without considering total intake and appetite.

### Benchmark scoring targets

- Does the system identify sarcopenia/frailty risk?
- Does it distinguish intentional fat loss from harmful lean-mass loss?
- Does it integrate HF symptom management, diabetes, and geriatric nutrition?
- Does it propose monitoring beyond weight and HbA1c?
