# SYN-012: HFpEF + obesity + T2D + sarcopenic risk

## Case summary

Mrs. J is a 76-year-old woman with HFpEF, obesity, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and early signs of sarcopenic risk. She recently lost weight after starting a GLP-1/GIP medication. Her shortness of breath improved slightly, but she now reports weakness, poor appetite, and difficulty rising from a chair.

## Social and demographic context

Mrs. J is widowed and lives alone in senior housing. Her daughter visits twice weekly and helps with transportation, shopping, and medication organization. She receives Meals on Wheels three days per week and buys groceries from a nearby dollar store and supermarket. She has limited income but stable housing.

She identifies as White and describes her diet as “old-fashioned American food”: toast, soup, sandwiches, casseroles, canned vegetables, chicken, meatloaf, potatoes, and occasional sweets. She dislikes protein shakes that taste “medicinal.”

## Medical history

- HFpEF with exertional dyspnea
- Type 2 diabetes for 14 years
- Obesity
- Hypertension
- Hyperlipidemia
- Osteoarthritis
- Recent functional decline
- History of falls

## Current medications

- Tirzepatide
- SGLT2 inhibitor
- Loop diuretic
- Beta blocker
- Statin
- Antihypertensive medication
- Vitamin D

## Dietary pattern

Breakfast is coffee and toast or nothing. Lunch is often soup, crackers, or a small sandwich. Dinner may be a Meals on Wheels tray, frozen meal, or a few bites of chicken and vegetables. She eats very small portions because she feels full quickly. Some days she skips meat because it feels heavy. She limits fluids when she notices swelling but does not know whether she has a prescribed fluid limit.

## Patient priorities

Mrs. J wants to keep losing weight because her breathing improved, but she is worried about weakness and falls. Her daughter asks whether she should drink protein shakes or reduce the medication dose. Mrs. J wants food ideas that are small, easy, affordable, and not too salty.

## Additional case details

- She cannot stand long enough to cook full meals.
- She has difficulty chewing tough meat.
- She has not done resistance training.
- She sometimes forgets lunch if no one reminds her.
- She has no clear written fluid or sodium plan from cardiology.
