## Case SYN-001 — Elena Maria: Type 2 diabetes with cardiometabolic risk and limited food access

### Case metadata
- **Case ID:** SYN-001
- **Synthetic status:** Fully synthetic
- **Primary evaluation focus:** Diabetes-related dietary modification, cardiometabolic risk, affordability, cultural fit, and access to care

### Patient profile
- **Age:** 35 years
- **Sex:** Female
- **Race/ethnicity:** Hispanic
- **Primary language:** English and Spanish
- **Occupation:** Works two sedentary jobs, often totaling about 12 hours/day
- **Living situation:** Lives with two children
- **Insurance:** Uninsured
- **Food access:** Urban area without a nearby full-service supermarket; primarily shops at a local bodega/convenience store

Elena presents with persistent fatigue and progressively worsening blurred vision over the past 6 months. She also reports increased thirst and frequent urination.

### Typical dietary pattern
Elena reports frequent use of convenience foods because of long workdays and limited time for food preparation.

**Typical weekday pattern**
- **Breakfast:** Sweetened coffee and a packaged pastry, or breakfast skipped
- **Mid-morning:** Fruit-flavored drink or sweetened coffee
- **Lunch:** Convenience-store sandwich, chips, or a fast-food meal
- **Afternoon:** Sweetened beverage and packaged snack
- **Dinner:** Rice, tortillas, beans, meat when available, and a prepared or convenience side dish
- **Evening:** Cookies, sweet bread, or another snack if working late

She reports regular intake of sugar-sweetened beverages, including fruit-flavored drinks and coffee with added sugar.

### Food access, cultural, and socioeconomic context
- Fresh produce is available inconsistently and often costs more than packaged foods
- Lean proteins are relatively expensive at nearby stores
- She is concerned that healthcare and RDN visits could take money away from her children's needs
- Family gatherings center around traditional meals
- She worries that being told to avoid tortillas or rice would feel like rejecting family and cultural traditions
- She was told she had “a touch of sugar” during pregnancy and believed it resolved after delivery

### Lifestyle
- Sedentary work
- No structured physical activity
- Long workdays limit meal preparation and exercise
- Sleep is often shortened by work and caregiving demands

### Patient goals and concerns
- Improve energy and reduce symptoms
- Learn how to change her current diet without giving up culturally important foods
- Find low-cost changes that can be made using foods available near home
- Avoid recommendations that require frequent specialty shopping or expensive products

### Benchmark-relevant complexity
This case requires the system to integrate current intake, diabetes-related risk, obesity, blood pressure, dyslipidemia, possible early kidney involvement, limited food access, cultural preferences, time constraints, and lack of insurance without defaulting to an idealized or expensive meal plan.
